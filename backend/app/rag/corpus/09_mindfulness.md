# Mindfulness-Based Approaches

## Overview
Mindfulness involves intentionally paying attention to the present moment, without judgment. It is a core skill underlying several evidence-based interventions, including Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) and Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT), the latter specifically developed to prevent depressive relapse.

## Core Practices
- **Breath awareness**: Anchoring attention on the sensation of breathing, gently returning attention when the mind wanders (which is expected and not a failure).
- **Body scan**: Systematically bringing attention to different areas of the body, noticing sensations without trying to change them.
- **Observing thoughts non-judgmentally**: Practicing noticing thoughts as mental events ("I'm having the thought that...") rather than facts, which creates psychological distance from distressing thoughts.
- **Mindful daily activities**: Bringing full attention to routine activities (eating, walking, washing dishes) as informal mindfulness practice.

## Why It Helps
Mindfulness practice is associated with reduced rumination, reduced reactivity to stress, and improved emotion regulation. For anxiety and depression specifically, it helps interrupt the automatic cycle of getting pulled into worry or rumination by building the capacity to notice a thought without immediately following it.

## Practical Notes
Mindfulness is a skill that develops with repeated practice, often starting with just a few minutes a day. It is not about "clearing the mind" or achieving total calm — moments of distraction are a normal and expected part of practice, not a sign of doing it wrong.
