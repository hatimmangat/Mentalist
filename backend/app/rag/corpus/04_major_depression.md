# Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

## Overview
Major Depressive Disorder involves a persistently low or depressed mood, or loss of interest/pleasure in previously enjoyed activities (anhedonia), lasting at least two weeks, along with additional symptoms that represent a change from previous functioning.

## Common Symptoms
Depressed mood most of the day, markedly diminished interest or pleasure in activities, significant weight or appetite change, sleep disturbance (insomnia or hypersomnia), psychomotor agitation or slowing, fatigue or loss of energy, feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, difficulty concentrating or making decisions, and recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation.

## Cognitive Patterns
Depression is often accompanied by a negative cognitive triad: negative views of oneself, the world, and the future. Common distortions include all-or-nothing thinking, overgeneralization, and personalization (blaming oneself for things outside one's control). Rumination — repetitively dwelling on distressing thoughts — tends to maintain and deepen depressive episodes.

## Supportive Approaches
Behavioral activation (gradually re-engaging in valued and pleasurable activities even before motivation returns) is one of the most effective and accessible interventions, because depression often creates a cycle where low activity deepens low mood. Cognitive restructuring, addressing sleep hygiene, and encouraging social connection (which depression often erodes) are also core components of evidence-based care.

## When to Encourage Professional Support
Persistent depressive symptoms lasting more than two weeks, especially with significant functional impairment, should be evaluated by a licensed clinician. Any mention of suicidal thoughts, a plan, or intent requires immediate escalation to crisis resources — this is addressed separately in the crisis-response protocol document.
