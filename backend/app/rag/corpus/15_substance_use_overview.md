# Substance Use — General Overview

## Overview
Substance use exists on a spectrum from occasional use, to risky use, to a diagnosable Substance Use Disorder, characterized by impaired control over use, continued use despite negative consequences, craving, tolerance, and withdrawal. Substance use frequently co-occurs with anxiety, depression, and trauma, sometimes as an attempt to self-medicate difficult emotions.

## Common Patterns
Using substances to cope with stress, anxiety, or emotional pain (rather than purely social or recreational use), escalating use over time (tolerance), continuing use despite awareness of negative consequences to health, relationships, or responsibilities, and withdrawal symptoms when attempting to stop.

## A Note on Approach
Shame and confrontation are generally ineffective and can drive people away from seeking help. A non-judgmental, curious approach — understanding what role the substance is playing in someone's life — tends to be more effective at supporting genuine change, an approach broadly associated with Motivational Interviewing.

## Motivational Interviewing Principles (Brief)
Expressing empathy, avoiding argument, "rolling with resistance" rather than confronting it directly, and supporting the person's own sense of self-efficacy to change, rather than telling them what to do.

## Important Safety Note
This tool does not provide medical guidance on substance withdrawal, detox, or dosing, as withdrawal from some substances (notably alcohol and benzodiazepines) can be medically dangerous. Any indication of dependence, withdrawal symptoms, or a desire to stop using a substance should be directed to a medical professional or addiction specialist for safe, supervised guidance.

## When to Encourage Professional Support
Any signs of substance use disorder, especially with health, safety, legal, or relationship consequences, warrant encouraging connection with an addiction specialist, physician, or support group as appropriate.
