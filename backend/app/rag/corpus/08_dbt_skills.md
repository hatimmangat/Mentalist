# DBT Skills — Distress Tolerance and Emotion Regulation

## Overview
Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) was originally developed for individuals experiencing intense, difficult-to-regulate emotions, and is now widely used for a range of emotional and behavioral challenges. It combines acceptance-based and change-based strategies across four skill modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness.

## Distress Tolerance (Surviving a Crisis Without Making It Worse)
- **TIPP**: Temperature (brief cold exposure to the face), Intense exercise, Paced breathing (slow exhale longer than inhale), Paired muscle relaxation — used to rapidly reduce extreme emotional arousal.
- **Distraction (ACCEPTS)**: Activities, Contributing, Comparisons, opposite Emotions, Pushing away, other Thoughts, Sensations — short-term strategies to tolerate a crisis until it passes.
- **Self-soothing through the five senses**: Deliberately engaging sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch in comforting ways.
- **Radical acceptance**: Fully acknowledging reality as it is, without approval, in order to reduce the added suffering that comes from fighting against what cannot currently be changed.

## Emotion Regulation
- **Identifying and labeling emotions** accurately, which reduces their intensity.
- **Opposite action**: Acting opposite to an unhelpful emotional urge when the emotion doesn't fit the facts (e.g., approaching rather than avoiding, when fear is disproportionate to actual danger).
- **PLEASE skills**: Treating Physical illness, balanced Eating, avoiding mood-Altering substances, balanced Sleep, getting Exercise — maintaining physical vulnerability factors that affect emotional stability.

## Interpersonal Effectiveness (Brief Note)
DEAR MAN (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, stay Mindful, Appear confident, Negotiate) is a structured framework for making requests or setting boundaries effectively while maintaining the relationship and self-respect.

## Application Note
DBT skills are typically most effective when learned within a structured DBT program including skills group and individual therapy, particularly for individuals experiencing very intense emotional swings or self-harm urges.
