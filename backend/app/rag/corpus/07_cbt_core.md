# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) — Core Techniques

## Overview
CBT is a structured, evidence-based approach built on the idea that thoughts, feelings, and behaviors are interconnected — changing unhelpful thought patterns and behaviors can improve emotional wellbeing. It is one of the most researched and effective approaches across anxiety, depression, and many other conditions.

## Cognitive Distortions (Common Unhelpful Thinking Patterns)
- **All-or-nothing thinking**: Seeing situations in black-and-white extremes with no middle ground.
- **Overgeneralization**: Taking one negative event as evidence of a never-ending pattern.
- **Catastrophizing**: Assuming the worst possible outcome will occur.
- **Mind reading**: Assuming you know what others are thinking, usually negatively, without evidence.
- **Fortune telling**: Predicting a negative future as if it were fact.
- **Personalization**: Blaming yourself for events outside your control.
- **Should statements**: Rigid rules about how you or others "should" behave, generating guilt or frustration.
- **Emotional reasoning**: Assuming that because you feel something, it must be true ("I feel like a failure, so I must be one").

## Cognitive Restructuring Process
1. Identify the automatic thought tied to a distressing emotion.
2. Identify which distortion(s) it reflects.
3. Examine the evidence for and against the thought.
4. Generate a more balanced, realistic alternative thought.
5. Notice the shift in emotional intensity.

## Behavioral Techniques
- **Behavioral activation**: Scheduling small, achievable, valued activities to counter withdrawal and low motivation.
- **Exposure**: Gradual, structured confrontation of avoided situations to reduce fear over time.
- **Activity monitoring**: Tracking mood alongside daily activities to identify patterns between behavior and emotional state.

## Application Note
These techniques are most effective when practiced consistently, ideally with guidance from a trained therapist, especially for more severe symptoms.
