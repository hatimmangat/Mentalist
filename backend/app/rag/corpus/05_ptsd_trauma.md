# PTSD and Trauma-Informed Support

## Overview
Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder can develop after exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, either directly, by witnessing it, or by learning it happened to a close family member or friend. Symptoms fall into four clusters: intrusion (flashbacks, nightmares, distressing memories), avoidance (of reminders, places, people, thoughts), negative alterations in mood and cognition (persistent negative beliefs, distorted blame, detachment), and alterations in arousal and reactivity (hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, irritability, sleep disturbance).

## Trauma-Informed Principles
When supporting someone who has experienced trauma, prioritize physical and emotional safety, transparency about what is happening and why, the person's choice and control over the conversation (never pressuring disclosure of details), and avoiding language that implies blame. Trauma responses are not a character flaw — they are the nervous system's adaptive, protective reaction to overwhelming experience.

## Grounding and Stabilization
For someone experiencing flashbacks or dissociation, grounding techniques that orient them to the present moment are helpful: naming five things they can see, feeling their feet on the floor, holding a cold object, or stating the current date and location out loud.

## Supportive Approaches
Trauma-focused CBT, EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing), and prolonged exposure therapy delivered by trained clinicians are the most evidence-supported treatments for PTSD. Peer or supportive listening should never attempt to replicate exposure therapy or push someone to relive traumatic content — that work belongs with a trained trauma specialist.

## When to Encourage Professional Support
Any disclosure of trauma symptoms interfering with daily life warrants encouraging professional evaluation, ideally from a trauma-informed clinician. This is especially important given the risk of symptoms worsening without proper support.
